## 1. Comparing two bottles

Two bottles can both have 60 capsules and cost the same, but if one lists two capsules per serving, it has half as many labeled servings as a bottle that lists one. Check the serving size before comparing the price. Here’s a worked example from Vera Health Notes.

## 2. Reading a blend label

If a label lists a 300 mg blend with three ingredients underneath, that number is for the blend as a whole. You can’t assume it means 100 mg of each. If you’re comparing labels, write down the blend total and leave the individual amounts blank when they aren’t shown. We put together a guide at Vera Health Notes with a few more examples.

## 3. Checking a marketplace seller

If you buy supplements online, look for both “Sold by” and “Ships from” before you check out. They can be different, and the seller name helps you find the return policy for that order. Vera Health Notes has a short walkthrough for when the name on the bottle and the name in your cart don’t match.

## 4. Getting ready for an appointment

One thing you can do before a visit is put prescriptions, over-the-counter products, and supplements on the same dated list. Include occasional products too, and add a question beside anything you’re unsure about. Here’s a simple format from Vera Health Notes that you can copy onto paper or into a phone note.
