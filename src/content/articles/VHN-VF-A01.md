# Why Are My Blood Pressure Readings Different One Minute Apart?

You sit down with your home blood pressure monitor, take a reading, wait a minute, and check again.

The numbers are different.

Does that mean your monitor is wrong? Not necessarily.

Blood pressure naturally changes, and the way a measurement is taken can influence the result. A single reading does not tell the whole story.

## Why two readings may differ

Your position, how long you rested, whether you were talking, and the fit of your cuff can all affect a measurement.

The American Heart Association recommends taking two readings one minute apart and recording the results.

That means a difference between two measurements is not automatically a reason to panic or assume your device is defective.

## Five things to check before measuring

1. Avoid smoking, caffeine, and exercise for at least 30 minutes beforehand.
2. Empty your bladder and sit quietly for at least five minutes.
3. Keep your back supported and your feet flat on the floor.
4. Support your arm at heart level.
5. Use a properly fitting cuff on bare skin.

Follow the instructions for your particular monitor.

## What should you do with different readings?

Record both measurements instead of choosing the number you prefer.

Include the date, time, readings, and anything unusual about the circumstances.

If you notice a pattern or have concerns about your results, discuss your record with your healthcare professional.

Do not stop or change prescribed blood pressure medication based on home measurements without medical guidance.

## Bring your monitor to your appointment

Consider bringing your monitor and your reading log to your next appointment.

Your healthcare team can help check your technique, review the device, and interpret the measurements in the context of your medical history.

## When to seek help

Home blood pressure monitoring does not replace professional care.

If you have a severely elevated reading or concerning symptoms, follow current emergency guidance from a qualified medical source. Do not delay emergency treatment to repeat measurements.
