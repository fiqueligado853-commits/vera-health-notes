The VitalFlush ad sends you to a page with a price per bottle and a money-back guarantee. Before you use either number, check which package the price describes and which policy goes with that page. On the public pages we reviewed, a buyer could see two different guarantee windows on the same domain.

On November 25, 2026, we found U.S. package prices of $89, $138, and $168 on one public Vital Flush sales page. Those figures came from the advertised packages. We did not place an order, verify a final charge, or confirm the terms attached to an individual receipt. The amounts below let you compare the page’s numbers on the same basis.

The three-bottle option had the lowest advertised price per bottle and the highest advertised package total. Relative to the two-bottle option, the three-bottle package adds $30 to the listed price. That is the difference between these two displayed packages before any separate adjustments. It is not a standalone price for one bottle or proof that a later order would receive the same terms.

If the ad you saw quotes a different price, note the number of bottles, the date, and the address of that page. A per-bottle figure can look like the amount due today when it actually applies only to a bundle. The [FTC’s online shopping guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping) recommends comparing the full cost, including shipping and other fees. Compare your final order summary to the package you selected before entering payment information.

## Does VitalFlush have a 60-day or 90-day refund window?

The [homepage](https://www.getvitalflush.com/) describes a 60-day window and refers to the order as its starting point. Its [shipping and returns page](https://www.getvitalflush.com/legal/shipping-returns.html) also describes a 60-day guarantee. A [separate offer page on that domain](https://www.getvitalflush.com/offer/) advertises 90 days and links to the shipping and returns policy. Both statements were visible in the materials reviewed. We cannot establish which guarantee a specific buyer would receive or whether the seller would honor a claim under either description.

A guarantee headline also leaves practical questions unanswered. Even if you know the number of days, ask whether the clock uses the order date or delivery date, what counts as making a request on time, and whether the seller requires a return authorization. Find out whether the seller asks for sealed bottles, opened bottles, or all units in a bundle. Ask whom to contact, whether you pay to ship anything back, and whether the refund includes original shipping. Those are questions for the seller and the written terms for your order.

If you have already bought the drops, start with your order confirmation, receipt, and saved order terms. Look for the seller and support route named there. Keep a record of the date and the specific policy you were shown. If two pages give different windows, send the relevant details to the seller and request written clarification. A search result with the same product name may describe a different storefront. The [VitalFlush reviews and seller check](/articles/vital-flush-reviews/) explains how to tell an affiliate page from the parties to the sale.

## Is the VitalFlush offer a subscription?

The homepage describes its packages as one-time purchases. The separate offer page also describes its listed packages as one-time purchases and instructs readers to review the terms of any separate optional offer. That language supports what each page says about its displayed package. It does not establish what every buyer sees, whether a separate offer appears, or what any later offer would authorize.

Check the selected frequency, the amount due today, and the wording next to the final confirmation button. If a screen offers something extra, treat that screen as a separate decision. Look for an additional charge, a renewal interval, the future price, and a way to decline the offer if you do not want it. If a site mentions a subscription, check how to cancel and when a cancellation must be made to affect the next order. Read the [FTC’s guide to free trials and auto-renewal](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions) for general steps to check a recurring offer.

If you see an unexpected charge after ordering, compare it with your receipt and any separate confirmation email before deciding what it was. Write down which purchase you recognize, the date and amount of the other charge, and the terms you actually saw. A refund request for an item already purchased and a request to stop future charges are different requests. If both apply, ask for confirmation of each. Keep your order and payment details private when describing the issue publicly.

## Match a refund promise to the page you actually used

The difference between the two published windows matters if you saved a screenshot of the headline but cannot find the policy attached to your order. Keep the original page address with the screenshot. If you already have an order confirmation, compare the addresses in that email with your notes. Ask which date starts the clock and which action the seller requires by the deadline. When a separate offer was accepted, ask whether its items have the same return rules as the main package.

For a written inquiry, describe the listing or order and quote the part that differs. A simple question such as “The offer page showed 90 days and the returns page showed 60. Which applies to my order, and how do I start a return?” gives support a specific question to answer. We did not receive an order-specific response that settles that conflict.

## A short comparison to make before you pay

Take a minute to put the answers on the same page. Write down the seller’s name, the number of bottles, the full amount due today, any future charges shown, and the written refund deadline for that order. Save the checkout summary and the policy that goes with it. If the seller sends clarification, save the reply next to the original wording and the time you received it.

Package size can also matter when comparing pages that use the same name. If the price is tied to a different form, bottle size, or storefront, keep it in a separate note.
