The product page says “money-back guarantee.” The order summary has a small line about delivery every 30 days. You may still have to open another page to find out who pays return shipping. Each line answers a different question. Before clicking pay, find the amount you owe today, any amount the seller may charge later, and what you would need to do to get a refund.

Keep the checkout page and the policy open side by side. Start with the selected package and seller. The terms on a review page, a separate storefront, or an older ad might apply to a different order. This guide is for U.S. shoppers and uses fictional examples so you can apply the steps to a specific listing yourself.

## Write down two prices

First write down what the seller would charge today. The [FTC’s online shopping guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping) suggests considering the full cost, including shipping and other fees. Make sure the amount still reflects the quantity, discount, destination, and delivery option you selected. The most prominent price on the product page may describe a single unit, a large bundle’s price per bottle, or an introductory order. Compare totals on the same basis.

Next write down any charge the order authorizes later. Look for phrases such as “subscribe and save,” “ships every 30 days,” or “renews at the regular price.” Read the line beside the final order button and any box that changes the delivery option. Switching from one-time purchase to a subscription can change both the amount due today and the future price. Check the selection again after you change the package size or apply a coupon.

Here is an example with made-up amounts. Assume both totals include the costs displayed at checkout.

| Question | One-time order | Subscription option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What would I pay today? | $44 | $36 |
| Would there be a scheduled charge? | None in this example | $44 every 30 days |
| What would two shipments cost? | $88 if I place a second identical order | $80 if the subscription continues through one renewal |

The lower first total might fit a shopper who already plans to reorder. A shopper who only intends to try one package might care more about when the renewal starts and how easily it can be stopped. If you cannot determine the future amount, date, or how to cancel, leave the subscription price undecided. A percentage discount cannot answer those questions.

## Open the policy and find the rules for your order

Find the policy linked near the order summary or identified in the terms for the seller you selected. Read it along with the guarantee displayed on the listing. Begin with four details.

1. **When the countdown starts.** Check whether the policy uses the order date, delivery date, or another event. Use the language the seller actually gives you.
2. **What condition the items must be in.** Look for rules about opened bottles, empty containers, seals, and how many items from a bundle must be returned.
3. **What counts as starting or finishing a return.** A policy may ask you to contact support, get a return authorization, dispatch the package, or have it arrive by a stated date. Write down the event it specifies.
4. **What amount comes back.** Look for original shipping, return postage, fees, bundle discounts, and which items the refund covers.

For example, suppose a fictional policy advertises a 30-day return window and says opened packages can be returned only with an authorization. If the seller has not explained how to request that authorization, the advertised window alone will not tell you how the return works. Ask the seller for the steps and whether the deadline applies to the request or to the parcel. Getting the details before ordering also tells you what evidence to keep.

A policy may name a support site you did not see on the ad. Check which company is responsible for this purchase before relying on the instructions. Our [guide to identifying the seller](/articles/how-to-tell-who-sells-a-supplement/) shows where to look for the names behind an ad, label, checkout, and receipt.

## Separate ending a subscription from returning a shipment

A return request addresses merchandise already ordered. Canceling a subscription addresses future charges or shipments. If you want both, look for both sets of steps. Some stores show cancellation controls in the order account. Others identify a support channel in the terms. Before paying, check when a change needs to be made, whether the next order might already be processing, and whether you get written confirmation.

The [FTC’s guide to free trials and auto-renewals](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions) recommends learning how to cancel before signing up. Keep a record of any cancellation and check statements afterward. When you contact support, make the request direct, for example, “Please confirm whether my subscription is canceled and whether any further charges are scheduled.” If also requesting a refund for a shipment, identify the shipment separately and keep the reply with your order records.

Be careful with a checkout that adds an item or offer after the main purchase. Read any additional offer as its own transaction. Check its price, whether it repeats, and whether you have to take action to decline it. The terms for the first bottle do not necessarily describe an extra order made through a later screen.

## Keep a record you can use if the page changes

Save the final order summary with the seller name, package, price, selected delivery option, and date. Save the applicable policy nearby. If the checkout showed automatic renewal, write down the first renewal date, amount, and cancellation instructions in the same place. Check the email receipt when it arrives for any difference in the amount or option you selected. For your own records, use screenshots that leave card data and account credentials out of view.

If something differs, use the support route confirmed for that order. Say what you selected and what the summary or statement shows. Keep response dates, order numbers, cancellation confirmations, and any tracking for returns. The FTC’s shopping guide also discusses ways to handle problems with online orders. Follow the guidance for the type of payment and the facts of your case. Dispute deadlines and options can differ, so check the specific instructions provided by your payment company if you need to contact it.

Here is the quick check before you close the tab. Can you point to today’s total, say whether future charges are scheduled, explain what a return requires, and identify whom to contact? If one answer is still missing, ask for that answer in writing before you pay. A written reply tied to the package you selected is more useful than a promise copied from an ad.
